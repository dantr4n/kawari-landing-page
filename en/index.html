This part is not fiction.

Kawari isn't real. The studies below are. Read the limitations too. Judging the strength of evidence is a skill you have to train yourself.

Kosmyna et al. · MIT Media Lab · arXiv 2025

Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task

Weakestbrain connectivity in the ChatGPT group, measured by EEG

Findings: 54 people wrote essays in three groups: brain only, search engine, ChatGPT. The brain-only group showed the strongest, most distributed networks; search sat in the middle; the ChatGPT group was weakest. ChatGPT users struggled to quote their own essays. Conversely, people who wrote on their own first and used ChatGPT afterwards showed higher connectivity.

Limitations: Not yet peer-reviewed, small sample (only 18 people in session four). Different connectivity doesn't mean “brain damage”, and it isn't a direct measure of learning.

Bastani et al. · PNAS · 2025

Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics

+48% → −17%practice with AI → exam without it

Findings: Nearly 1,000 high school students in Turkey. The group with a ChatGPT-style interface did 48% better on practice problems, but once the AI was removed they scored 17% lower on the exam than students who never had it. AI users also overestimated how much they'd learned. A “tutor” version with guardrails — hints instead of answers — largely removed the harm.

Limitations: One subject (maths), one setting, short term. And the important part: the same AI, designed differently, gave a different result.

Shen & Tamkin · Anthropic · 2026

How AI Impacts Skill Formation

−17%comprehension quiz score in the AI-assisted group

Findings: A randomized trial with 52 (mostly junior) developers learning a new Python library. The AI group scored about 17% lower on the quiz right afterwards, with the biggest gap in debugging; they were only slightly faster, and not significantly. People who used the AI to ask conceptual questions and request explanations retained more.

Limitations: Small sample, one short task, programming. And yes — this is Anthropic's own study, the company that makes the AI that wrote this page.

Fan et al. · British Journal of Educational Technology · 2025

Beware of Metacognitive Laziness: Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Learning Motivation, Processes, and Performance

Findings: A randomized experiment with students on a writing task: the ChatGPT group improved their essay scores, but didn't gain significantly more knowledge or transfer it better. They also planned and self-evaluated less than the group guided by human experts — the authors call it “metacognitive laziness”.

Limitations: One writing task, short duration, knowledge measured right after the session.

Lee et al. · CHI 2025 · Microsoft Research & Carnegie Mellon

The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers

319knowledge workers, 936 real examples

Findings: The more people trusted the AI, the less critical thinking they did. The more confident they were in their own ability, the more critical thinking they did.

Limitations: Self-reported, cross-sectional survey: shows an association, doesn't prove cause.

Gerlich · Societies · 2025

AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking

666participants (real number, we didn't pick it)

Findings: Frequent AI users scored lower on critical thinking, largely through offloading their thinking to the tool. Younger participants depended on AI more and scored lower.

Limitations: Correlational, doesn't prove cause. The paper received a correction in September 2025.

Budzyń et al. · The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology · 2025

Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy

28.4% → 22.4%adenoma detection rate without AI

Findings: 19 experienced endoscopists in Poland. After getting used to an AI polyp detector, their detection rate in exams done without it dropped. These were experts, not beginners.

Limitations: Observational, retrospective, and contested; a 2026 prospective study in Norway found no significant change after the AI was removed.

Dahmani & Bohbot · Scientific Reports · 2020

Habitual use of GPS negatively impacts spatial memory during self-guided navigation

Findings: 50 drivers: more lifetime GPS use went with worse spatial memory when navigating unaided. 13 were retested about three years later: more GPS use in between went with a steeper decline.

Limitations: Very small follow-up sample. It's GPS, not AI — but the same kind of handing the job to a machine.

Roediger & Karpicke · Psychological Science · 2006

Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention

The good newstrying to recall beats re-reading

Findings: Re-reading wins if the test comes 5 minutes later. Self-testing — trying to recall without looking — wins when the test comes 2 days or a week later.

Limitations: The material was science passages, not a foreign language. But this effect has been replicated many times, across many kinds of material.

None of these measured Japanese learning specifically. The mechanism is the same though: what you never pull out of your own memory, you don't keep.

No teacher, no app, no AI. Including the AI that wrote this page.

The AI isn't the monster. The habit of handing it all your thinking is.

Use it anyway, but in this order:

  1. Recall first, look up second.Ran into 「紫陽花」? Guess the reading before you switch on furigana. Effortful recall is what writes the memory.
  2. Draft it yourself, let AI fix it after.Write your own sentence with 〜ざるを得えない — wrong is fine. Then ask for corrections, and explain to yourself why it was wrong.
  3. Ask “why”, not just “what”.Don't just ask how 「山葵」 is read. Ask why it's read わさび, and you'll understand 熟字訓じゅくじくん. People who asked to understand concepts kept more than people who only asked for answers.
  4. Write by hand. Say it out loud. Space your reviews.Slower. More annoying. Works better. 継続けいぞくは力ちからなり.
